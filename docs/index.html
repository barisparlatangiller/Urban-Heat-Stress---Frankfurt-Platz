How hot do our squares get?

Felt temperature on 24 public squares, simulated for every hour of the 24–28 June 2026 heatwave (air up to 40.8 °C) at 1 m detail.

How to read this map
  1. Colours show how hot it feels to stand there. Sun, hot paving and warm walls add to the air temperature; shade is the cool blue-green.
  2. Drag the timeline at the bottom, or press ▶, to watch shadows and heat move through the day.
  3. Click a square on the map or in the list to see its daily heat profile.
  4. Turn on 3D city or the aerial photo to see what makes a place hot or cool.

Hours per day (09–19 h) an average spot on the square spends in very strong heat stress — feeling like 38 °C or more.

    Method. SOLWEIG radiation model (UMEP) on a 1 m grid. Times are summer time (CEST).

    Limits. Air temperature and wind come from the airport station and are applied to the whole area; the city centre is usually warmer and less windy, so felt temperatures are likely conservative. Not yet validated with field measurements. Values describe a standing adult outdoors.

    Scope. An independent research project built only from openly available data. Some factors are not captured, for example temporary shade (awnings, umbrellas), fountains, private and courtyard trees, green roofs and façades. Read the results as an informed estimate, not as official measurements.
